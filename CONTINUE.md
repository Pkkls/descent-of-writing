Then take ONE item from the ranked list in CONTINUE.md. Do not take several.
Each one changes what the plate asserts, and they need to be reviewable

Rules that are not negotiable on this project:

- Every number on the plate is computed from a source file and asserted in
  verify.py. If you add a claim, add its check. After the check passes, break
  the thing it checks and confirm it goes red. A check that cannot fail is
  not a check.
- Never draw a glyph you did not pull from a font and shape with HarfBuzz. A
  script with no font gets a marked gap, never a lookalike.
- Never relabel "no script recorded" as "unwritten". The distinction is the
  most honest thing on the plate and it is easy to erode in a caption.
- Keep the edge-strength tags. If you cannot source a descent claim, mark it
  contested rather than promoting it to attested.
- Report what you actually observed. If you changed genealogy.py, say which
  rows and on what authority.

Settled, do not reopen without a reason:
- The pyramid silhouette was traded for a tidy tree on purpose. Centring each
  depth band severs a node from its parent and the descent lines become
  untraceable sweeps.
- Descent is curated by hand, not scraped. Wikidata P144 covers about half the
  registry and contradicts itself on the Semitic chain.
- Zsye (Emoji) stays on the plate, typed as a symbol set, because the scope
  removes only the six named Z codes and a silent edit is worse.

## Open work, ranked

### 1. The stimulus edges are a default, not research

56 edges are tagged `stimulus`. **37 of them point at Latin, and 29 of those
are twentieth-century inventions.** That parent was applied broadly rather than
established per script, and several are probably wrong:

- `Cans` Canadian Aboriginal Syllabics is standardly derived from Devanagari
  and Pitman shorthand, not from Latin.
- `Plrd` Pollard Miao draws on Latin **and** Cree syllabics.
- `Brai` Braille descends from Barbier's night writing.
- `Sgnw` SignWriting comes out of Sutton DanceWriting.
- `Ogam` Ogham's relationship to Latin is genuinely disputed.
- `Sora` Sora Sompeng is claimed by its community as revealed, not derived.

Go through all 37 in `genealogy.py`, source each one, and either fix the parent
or keep it and say why. This is the highest-value item on the list: it is the
central claim of the plate and it is the weakest part of it.

### 2. The eleven contested edges need a specialist

Named, so they can be argued with rather than found by accident:

`Aghb`<-`Armn`, `Armn`<-`Grek`, `Brah`<-`Armi`, `Cari`<-`Grek`,
`Geok`<-`Grek`, `Hang`<-`Phag`, `Hung`<-`Orkh`, `Mtei`<-`Brah`,
`Orkh`<-`Sogd`, `Tfng`<-`Sarb`, `Yiii`<-`Hani`

The Aramaic origin of Brahmi and the Phags-pa influence on Hangul are the two
that carry the most weight downstream. Brahmi alone governs the placement of
about 60 scripts.

### 3. Language to script coverage is 17.7%

The largest single gap in the plate. Wikidata P282 and CLDR together reach
1,518 of 8,587 languages, which is why 82.3% land in the unrecorded register.

Worth trying, in order: ScriptSource (scriptsource.org) publishes
language-to-script data; Wikipedia's per-language infoboxes are scrapeable and
better covered than Wikidata's structured claims; Ethnologue has it but is
paywalled and its licence does not permit redistribution.

Any improvement here changes Zone B materially. Whatever the number becomes,
keep the label honest: it is still what sources record, not what is true.

### 4. No CI, so upstream drift is invisible

`verify.py` exists and nothing runs it. A GitHub Action on a monthly schedule
that runs the full pipeline and fails on a changed count would catch new ISO
15924 codes and new Glottolog releases the week they land, instead of the plate
silently becoming a 2026 snapshot that claims to be current.

The plate already prints its retrieval date, so the failure mode is staleness
rather than a false claim, but staleness compounds.

### 5. Seven specimens are recoverable

`Gara` `Gukh` `Krai` `Onao` `Sunu` `Todr` `Tutg` are encoded in Unicode 16 and
have no Noto face yet. Re-running `fonts.py` will pick them up as Noto ships
them, taking the specimen count from 179 to 186. The other 32 gaps are scripts
Unicode has never encoded, so no font for them can exist and they stay gaps.

### 6. The two recursions are not equal

Script descent is a full tree. Language descent is reduced to a sort key inside
each field. Script and genealogy are orthogonal (Latin script covers French,
Vietnamese, Turkish and Indonesian, which share no ancestor), and the plate
currently only shows one of them properly.

Drawing both trees facing each other, linked by chords, is denser and more
honest. It is also a different plate, so it may deserve to be a second output
rather than a change to this one.

### 7. The SVG is not usable in a browser

25,922 pixels wide. Browsers will open it, but nobody can navigate it, and the
6,000-wide PNG makes every card illegible. A deep-zoom viewer with tiles, or a
simple pan-and-zoom page with the SVG inline, would make the artefact actually
readable by people who are not going to download 1.7 MB and open Inkscape.

The SVG already carries an id on every node, so a viewer can link to a single
script.

### 8. Smaller, real, unglamorous

- 350 languages have no endangerment status. Glottolog simply lacks it for
  them; they currently render at the lowest opacity, which reads as "nearly
  extinct" rather than "unknown". That is a small lie in the encoding.
- Only 111 of 218 scripts have a native name. Wikidata P1705 is thin.
- Speaker counts reach 19.7% of languages. Mark area therefore encodes
  "unknown" as "smallest" for four languages in five.
- The plate has no `<title>` or `<desc>` elements, so it is opaque to a screen
  reader. For a document that is entirely about writing systems, that is a
  poor look.

## What a good change looks like

One item. A commit that says which rows moved and on what authority. A check in
`verify.py` that would have caught the old state. A note in the README if the
headline numbers moved.
